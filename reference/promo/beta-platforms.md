# Beta-Reader & ARC Recruitment Platform Reference

Platform characteristics and post templates for **recruiting** beta readers and ARC
reviewers — not for selling a finished book. Used by the `beta-promo-writer` skill.

**Framing difference from `platforms.md` (sales/launch promo):** every post here is an
*ask*, not a *pitch*. The CTA is "apply / DM / comment to get considered," never "buy
now" or a purchase link. Tone is grateful and low-pressure — the reader is being asked
for unpaid work (an honest review or structured feedback), not being sold something.

**Verify before posting, every time:** community rules for self-promotion, ARC/beta
requests, and required flairs or weekly threads change without notice and vary by
community. Nothing below is a claim that a specific rule currently holds for a named
subreddit/group — confirm the live rules/pinned post of the actual community
immediately before posting, not from this reference.

---

## Reddit

**Characteristics:**
- Most book-community subreddits gate self-promotion — a dedicated weekly
  self-promo/ARC thread, a required post flair, or a minimum "non-promotional
  participation" ratio (commonly discussed as roughly 9:1) are common patterns.
  **Read the sidebar/wiki rules of the specific subreddit right before posting** —
  do not assume any of these patterns apply without checking.
- Genre-specific subs (fantasy, dark fantasy, LGBTQ+ book communities, romance,
  horror, etc.) tend to be more receptive to ARC/beta requests than general subs —
  but still gate them the same way.
- Comments read as more authentic than link posts. Engage in the community before
  asking for anything.
- Never mass-post the identical text across multiple subreddits in one sitting —
  reads as spam and gets removed/banned.
- Post length: ~150–300 words reads well for a self-promo-thread comment or a
  dedicated ARC post; longer risks getting skipped in a thread of many requests.

**Post template:**
```markdown
## ARC / Beta Reader Request (self-promo-thread or ARC-flair post)

Hi all — I'm looking for [N] beta readers / ARC reviewers for **[Title]**,
a [genre] [book type] releasing [timeframe/TBD].

**What it's about:** [1–2 sentence pitch, no spoilers]

**Comp titles:** [if genuinely comparable — never force a comp]

**What I'm looking for:**
- Comfortable with [content notes if genre carries heavy themes]
- Can commit to reading by [deadline]
- [Optional: active reviewer on Goodreads/StoryGraph]

**How to apply:** [DM me / comment below / link to StoryOrigin application —
only include a link if the subreddit's rules allow it]

No obligation beyond an honest [review / feedback form] — thank you for reading!
```

## Goodreads

**Characteristics:**
- Genre-specific groups (often organized around tropes, cross-cutting identities
  like LGBTQ+, or "ARC swap" communities) run dedicated ARC-request threads —
  find the group's own thread/folder for this rather than posting to the general
  board.
- Goodreads readers who already review are a stronger recruitment target than
  cold outreach — their profile shows review history.
- Group etiquette is stricter than Reddit about repeat self-promotion — read each
  group's rules/pinned post before requesting.
- Post length: ~150–250 words — enough for a real pitch without reading as a wall
  of text in a thread of many requests.

**Post template:**
```markdown
## ARC Request — [Title]

**[Title]** by [Author] — [genre] [book type], releasing [timeframe/TBD].

[2–3 sentence pitch]

Looking for [N] ARC readers willing to post an honest review around release.
Comfortable with [content notes if applicable]? I'd love to have you.

Request via: [StoryOrigin link / DM — per group rules]
```

## StoryGraph

**Characteristics:**
- Smaller, faster-growing, mood/pace-tag-driven community — readers self-select
  by content warnings and mood tags rather than star ratings, which makes it a
  strong fit for genre with heavy or specific content.
- As of this reference's writing, StoryGraph has no large centralized ARC-group
  infrastructure comparable to Goodreads groups — recruitment tends to happen via
  profile posts, StoryGraph's own community spaces, or by cross-posting a
  StoryOrigin/BookFunnel link from other platforms. **Verify the current state of
  StoryGraph's community features before relying on this** — this platform's
  tooling has been evolving quickly.
- Readers here respond well to accurate mood/pace/content-warning framing — lean
  into StoryGraph's own vocabulary (mood, pace, content warnings) rather than a
  generic pitch.
- Post length: aim for roughly the same range as a Goodreads post (~150–250 words)
  — no platform-enforced limit, but shorter reads better in a feed context.

**Post template:**
```markdown
## ARC / Beta Reader Call — [Title]

**[Title]** — [genre] · Mood: [e.g. dark, tense, hopeful] · Pace: [e.g. medium]

[2–3 sentence pitch, mood-forward]

Content warnings: [honest list]

Looking for readers who want an early look before [timeframe]. Apply here:
[StoryOrigin link]
```

## TikTok / BookTok

**Characteristics:**
- Frame it as "help me test this before it's real" — BookTok rewards process
  transparency (unpolished, behind-the-scenes energy), not a sales pitch.
- Short-form video: face-to-camera or text overlay works; hook in the first
  2 seconds or viewers scroll past.
- CTA is "comment/DM to apply," not a purchase link — BookTok viewers are used to
  giveaway/ARC-style calls and respond well to them when they read as genuine.
- Don't oversell — a video that promises more than the book delivers burns trust
  with a community that talks to itself constantly.

**Script template:**
```markdown
## BookTok ARC/Beta Call

Format: Face-to-camera or text overlay on cover/aesthetic background
Hook (first 2s): "I need [N] people to read my book before anyone else does"
Body (10–20s): [genre, vibe, one emotional hook — NOT a plot summary]
CTA: "Comment '[emoji/keyword]' or link in bio to apply — free copy, just want
     honest thoughts"
```

## Facebook

**Characteristics:**
- Genre reader groups (often organized around identity/genre combos, e.g. LGBTQ+
  fantasy readers) are the main venue — same rule as Goodreads: read the group's
  own posting rules first, many require mod approval for promo posts.
- Longer, personal-story framing performs better than a terse ask — explain why
  this book, why now, why you need readers.
- Groups are worth more than a public page post for recruitment specifically.
- Post length: ~200–350 words — Facebook groups tolerate longer, story-forward
  posts better than Reddit/Goodreads threads do.

**Post template:**
```markdown
## ARC / Beta Reader Post (group)

[Personal 2–4 sentence intro — why this book, why this moment]

I'm looking for [N] beta readers / ARC reviewers for **[Title]**
([genre], releasing [timeframe/TBD]).

[Pitch, 2–3 sentences]

Comfortable with [content notes]? Can commit to reading by [deadline]?
Comment or DM me — thank you for even considering it!
```

## Bluesky

**Characteristics:**
- Small, chronological, author-community-heavy — a direct, unpolished ask reads
  better here than a polished campaign post.
- 300 character limit — keep the ask to one clear sentence plus a CTA.
- Good for the "building an ARC team" announcement; weaker for volume recruitment
  than Reddit/Facebook groups.

**Post template:**
```markdown
## ARC/Beta Call

Looking for [N] beta readers for [Title] ([genre]) — [one-line hook]. Free early
copy, just want honest feedback by [deadline]. Interested? [link/DM]
```

## Newsletter / Email List

**Characteristics:**
- Highest-quality recruitment channel if the author already has a list — these
  readers already opted in and are the most likely to actually finish and respond.
- Personal, direct tone; explain the ask plainly, no marketing voice.
- Good venue for the mid-point check-in reminder too, not just recruitment.

**Email template:**
```markdown
Subject: Want an early look at [Title]?

[Personal opening — why this book, why you're asking]

I'm putting together a small beta/ARC team for **[Title]**, out [timeframe/TBD].
No experience needed — just honest thoughts by [deadline].

Interested? [Apply link / reply to this email]

Thank you either way — this list is the reason I get to keep writing.
```

## Screening-Question Bank

Pick 3–5 per campaign — more reads as a barrier, not a filter.

- "What's your favorite [genre] book/series, and why?" — reveals actual genre fit
  better than a yes/no genre question.
- "Are you comfortable with [specific content warning relevant to this book]?" —
  ask directly for genre with heavy/dark content; never soften this to inflate
  signups.
- "Can you commit to finishing by [deadline]?" — filters for realistic capacity.
- "Are you active on Goodreads or StoryGraph?" (optional, if reviews matter for
  this campaign — Beta Copies feedback-only campaigns can skip this)
- **Series only:** "Have you read book [N-1]?" or "Are you open to reading book 1
  first?" — prevents readers from starting mid-series unknowingly.
- "Have you beta-read or ARC-reviewed before?" (optional) — helps calibrate how
  much guidance a reader needs, not a filter for rejecting first-timers.
- "Do you prefer reading on [device/format], and does that work for how this copy
  will be delivered?" — catches format mismatches before they cause drop-off.
- "How would you like to submit feedback — form, email, or direct comments in the
  file?" — sets the channel expectation up front instead of guessing later.

## Feedback-Form Question Bank

Core questions, genre/structure-conditional:

- **Tone-shift check** (if the book has a deliberate genre/tone shift): "At what
  point did the book feel like it changed for you?"
- **Character-surprise check:** "Which character surprised you most, and how?"
- **Series-specific** (if the book is part of a planned series):
  - "Do you want to know what happens next?"
  - "What are you most curious about for future books?"
  - "What open question is bothering you the most right now?"
- **Memoir-specific** (replace character-surprise with):
  - "Where did the book feel most true to your own experience?"
  - "Was there a moment that felt distant or held-back, rather than honest?"
